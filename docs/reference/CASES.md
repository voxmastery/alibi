# ALIBI — Real Cases, Dated

Every case below is from a court order, a PIB release, or named press reporting. Use the exact figures. **If a judge asks for a source, name the court and case number** — that's what separates you from teams quoting vibes.

⚠️ Verify anything you plan to state as fact on stage. Court citations are reliable; press reports of ongoing investigations can be revised.

---

## TIER 1 — Open with these

### 1. The Ludhiana furnace industrialists — the buyers' revolt
**~40 industrialists, Ludhiana · Induction Furnace Association of North India**

About 40 industrialists received Central and state GST notices over scrap dealers' bogus billing. They had already paid 18% GST to those dealers when procuring scrap. Association general secretary Dev Dutta's position: a few bad actors in the trade were already on the department's radar, but genuine industry was being served notices anyway — and since the department issues the GST numbers, verifying credentials is the department's duty, given buyers provide e-way bills, vehicle numbers and photographs.

**Why it's your opening:** this is not one aggrieved firm. It is an entire industry association saying *we did the paperwork and still got hit.* Exactly Alibi's user.

---

### 2. Jyoti Tar Products Pvt Ltd v. Deputy Commissioner, State Tax, Shibpur Charge
**Calcutta High Court · IA No. CAN/1/2024 · 14 January 2025**

Demand under Section 74 raised because the supplier's registration was cancelled retrospectively. The court found the department had not properly assessed invoices, e-way bills, bank statements or GSTR-2A records. Demand order quashed, matter remanded.

The appellant's core contention: **all purchases were made when the suppliers' registrations were active.**

Note the timeline in the file — the writ petition was dismissed on 11 November 2024 before the appeal succeeded in January 2025.

**Use for:** "the courts agree with the buyer — eventually."

---

### 3. Himalaya Communication Pvt Ltd v. Union of India
**Himachal Pradesh High Court · CWP No. 8809 of 2025 · 6 June 2025**

The petitioner had paid GST to the supplier, held valid tax invoices, and had verified that the supplier discharged its tax liability as evidenced in GSTR-3B. ITC was denied anyway — on the sole ground that the supplier's registration was later cancelled retrospectively.

The court held authorities must examine the documents before denying ITC under Section 16(2); that exercise wasn't undertaken. Orders dated **10 January 2025** and **31 March 2024** were set aside and remanded.

**Use this one for the cruelty of the timeline:** the first adverse order was March 2024. Relief came June 2025. **Fifteen months.** That's the cost of not having the record ready.

---

### 4. Niranjan Paul v. Assistant Commissioner of State Tax, Siliguri Charge
**Calcutta High Court · WPA 707 of 2025 · 8 April 2025**

ITC availed on supplies from Global Bitumen in **March 2019**. The supplier's registration was later cancelled retrospectively and the supplier alleged non-existent at its declared address. Denial under Section 73(9) with interest and penalty; appellate authority affirmed.

The petitioner produced invoices, e-way bills, RTGS payments, ledger accounts, a GSTR-2A match, and the supplier's own GSTR-1 and 3B filings. Authorities failed to consider any of it. Remanded.

**Use for the single most damning number in your pitch: transaction March 2019 → relief April 2025. Six years.**

## TIER 2 — The fraud structures (your graph edges)

### 5. Delhi ₹347 crore fake billing scam — the design spec
**Delhi State GST Department · announced 1 January 2022 · 11 firms · 1 arrest**

Circular trading and fake bills to fraudulently claim ITC. Investigators found across the 11 firms:
- **4 filed returns from computers sharing the same IP address**
- **6 shared the same contact number**
- **4 used the same email ID**
- **2 shared the same bank account number**
- On secret verification by department inspectors, **none of the 11 existed at their registered address**

Several firms belonged to one person, apparently set up to create long tax chains to avoid detection. Estimated evasion close to ₹40 crore.

**This is literally your risk rule set.** Say so: "I didn't invent these signals. This is what the Delhi GST department found."

### 6. Thane — 22 firms on stolen identity
**CGST Thane · PIB**

One individual registered **22 non-genuine firms** using misappropriated Aadhaar and PAN details, availing ₹48 crore and passing on ₹44 crore in fraudulent ITC.

### 7. Thane — ₹140 crore racket
**CGST Thane Commissionerate, CGST Mumbai Zone · 12 February 2025 · 1 arrest**

Over **18 dummy entities** created solely to defraud. ₹140 crore in fraudulent transactions, designed to avail ₹26.92 crore of ITC. The accused misused Aadhaar, PAN and other KYC documents **obtained from individuals in exchange for money**.

### 8. Lucknow — 131 fake firms, 1,100 victims downstream
**CGST Lucknow · PIB**

131 fake non-existent firms showed supplies worth **₹1,355.74 crore to roughly 1,100 recipient firms** across Uttar Pradesh, Haryana and Delhi, passing on ₹197.20 crore of ineligible credit — **without a single unit of goods actually moving.**

**This is the number that makes your point:** 131 fraudsters, **1,100 downstream buyers** now exposed. The fraud is small. The blast radius isn't.

### 9. Ludhiana — ₹455 crore, iron sector
**CGST Ludhiana Commissionerate · 2 arrests (a man and his son)**

Three related firms availed bogus invoices and utilised **₹69.41 crore** of ineligible ITC to set off GST liabilities.

### 10. Palghar and elsewhere
70 shell firms built around ₹320 crore in fake invoices (Palghar). Separately, companies with no real business activity generated ₹266 crore of fake invoices and used circular trading to inflate turnover before claiming ₹48 crore in fraudulent ITC.

## TIER 3 — Identity theft (use if you need emotional weight)

### 11. Srinivasa Rao, barber, Vijayawada — ₹72 lakh
**Reported April 2026**

Running a salon at Panja Centre for 40 years, earning ₹300–400 a day. Accused of operating a scrap iron business in Visakhapatnam. He found out in **May 2021** when a ward secretariat told him he was ineligible for welfare schemes because records showed him running a business and paying GST. **Six years** of notices followed. His bank account was frozen for non-payment. His PAN and Aadhaar had been misused; the phone number and email on the fraudulent records were not his.

### 12. Ajmer Singh, labourer, Moga — ₹35 crore
A ₹21 lakh notice in 2022, which he reported to the Ludhiana GST office with no action taken. Two years later, a **₹35 crore** notice. A fraudulent company, Cee Kay International, had been registered on his Aadhaar and PAN, shown as operating from Gill Road industrial area, Ludhiana.

### 13. Hyderabad — ₹32.57 crore on a stolen identity
Reported 9 September 2026.

**Handle these with care.** They're powerful but they're a *different* user than yours — Alibi protects the buyer, not the identity-theft victim. Use them for one line on how easy fake registrations are, then move on. Don't imply you solve them.

## TIER 4 — The settled law (for the Q&A)

Judgments holding that ITC cannot be denied to a bona fide buyer solely because of the supplier's retrospective cancellation:

| Suresh Trading Company | Supreme Court | 1998 |
| LGW Industries Ltd v. Assistant Commissioner | Calcutta HC | MAT 855 of 2022 / 2021 (12) TMI 834 |

| Engineering Tools Corporation | Madras HC | 2024 |
| Jyoti Tar Products | Calcutta HC | 14 Jan 2025 |
| Solvi Enterprises | Allahabad HC | 2025 (3) TMI 1313 |
| Niranjan Paul | Calcutta HC | WPA 707 of 2025, 8 Apr 2025 |
| Himalaya Communication | Himachal Pradesh HC | CWP 8809 of 2025, 6 Jun 2025 |
| Kesarwani Traders v. State of UP | Allahabad HC | 2025 (8) TMI 1132 |
| Shyamalmay Paul v. Assistant Commissioner SGST | Calcutta HC | 2025 (11) TMI 428, Dec 2025 |

**Still live:** the Supreme Court has the question before it — SLP 31296/2025, order dated **10 November 2025**, on whether ITC can be denied solely because the seller's registration was cancelled despite being valid on the transaction date. Notice returnable December 2025.

That case's own history is your best argument: Calcutta HC MAT 854 of 2024 (7 May 2024) quashed the original order for breach of natural justice → on remand the adjudicating authority **reiterated the same demand** → MAT 1212 of 2025 (28 August 2025) sent the petitioner back to statutory appeal → now before the Supreme Court.

**Say this if challenged on whether the problem is real:** "This question is currently before the Supreme Court. It is not settled enough that a buyer can afford to be without evidence."

### The policy position nobody enforces
A GST Council press release dated **4 May 2018** stated there shall be no automatic reversal of ITC from the buyer on non-payment of tax by the seller — recovery is to be made from the seller, and reversal from the buyer only in exceptional situations such as a missing dealer or closure of business.

**Eight years on, buyers still get the notice first.**

## Discipline

- **Don't stack all thirteen.** Three cases, well told, beat a wall of citations. Recommended: Ludhiana association → Niranjan Paul's six years → Delhi ₹347 crore for the graph.
- **Round carefully.** ₹1,355.74 crore, not "about fourteen hundred crore." Precision is the point.
- **Never claim Alibi would have prevented the fraud.** It would have shortened the defence. That's a smaller claim and a true one — and a judge who catches you overclaiming stops believing the rest.
